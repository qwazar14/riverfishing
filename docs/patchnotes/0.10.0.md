## Tackle & Fishing

### Fly fishing

*Spinning is a rhythm, float and bottom are a wait, the fight is a tug-of-war. A fly is none of those: it is timing, reading and restraint — and now it is its own way to fish.*

- **The Fly Rod** — bamboo, string and an iron nugget; takes a small reel (1000–2000) as its drag and a built-in **Fly Rig**: a leader for the tippet and a slot that takes only a fly you tied on the Tackle Station's Tie page. The same tied lure is a mormyshka through the ice and a fly here.
- **The cast is a rhythm.** Hold use and a needle sweeps a bar; the two ends are the two stops of a fly cast. Tap sneak on each end for a false cast and a block more line; release on a beat for a tight loop. Off the beat is a splash that spooks the water; well past the end is a wind knot and line wear. Distance is aim.
- **The drift.** Twelve seconds; on flowing water the fly goes with the current and the line bows and drags — the tension slot shows it, and a dragging fly is refused. **Left-click mends.** A third mend in one drift spooks; a line straight below you catches nothing, so pick up and cast again.
- **The rise.** The fish is drawn coming up under the fly, nose first, with a ring on the water — and **the strike bar is a clock, not a sweep**: the marker climbs once across the window and the green is the species' own delay. Lift before it and you pulled the fly out of its mouth; after it and the fish spat it. Every miss puts the fish down for twenty seconds.
- **The hatch.** Midges in winter, stoneflies on spring days, mayflies at dusk, caddis on summer evenings, ants on a clear summer afternoon, scuds in still water, fry in autumn and under a storm. When a hatch is on, the water shows it — motes over the surface and rise rings from fish that are not yours. **Match it:** the right kind of fly within 3 mm is ×1.5, the right kind at the wrong size ×1.0, the wrong kind ×0.6. With nothing hatching a dry fly is ×0.7, a streamer ×0.9 and a nymph ×1.0 — nymphs catch most fish most of the time.
- The backcast needs no room behind you yet, and there is no roll cast: those come with the second phase, with the strip-set and the indicator.

## Species

### 267 species, from one table
The species live in a spreadsheet now (`tools/import_species_xlsx.py` reads it): 160 new fish join the 107 — the North American sunfishes, basses, crappies, gars, catfishes and sturgeons, the African cichlids, bichirs, lungfishes and tigerfish, the Asian mahseers, barbels, tilapias and snakeheads, the South American stingrays, characins and knifefishes, and 30 hybrids. Every old species got its description and parameters reviewed.

- **Six new families**: panfish, catfishes, cichlids, characins, rays and exotics, filed in the journal beside the old ones.
- **Diet**: every species is a predator, an omnivore, peaceful or an insect feeder. The journal shows it; the fly a species takes is chosen by it.
- **Afrotropical**, the fifth faunal province — carved out of a third of the Neotropic and Indomalaya cells, so an existing world keeps its Palearctic and Nearctic exactly where they were.
- **Hybrids breed**: a cross the table names (tiger muskie, splake, saugeye, wiper, bester, the tilapia and sunfish crosses…) spawns that hybrid in the tank instead of the mother's species.

### What a species no longer asks for
The ideal rod, rig, reel size and reel tolerance, the cast distance band and the hook tolerance are gone from the profiles and from the bite engine. A species asks for bait, feed, line and hook size; how you deliver them is your business. The match is bait 45 %, groundbait 20 %, line 20 %, hook 15 %.

### Angler level
The ladder runs **0–50** now (the old 0–12 stretched, the new species placed beside their nearest old neighbours). The level never forbids a fish: short of the rung, the bite thins in proportion down to a floor of 15 %.
