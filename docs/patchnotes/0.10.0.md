## Tackle & Fishing

### Fly fishing

*Spinning is a rhythm, float and bottom are a wait, the fight is a tug-of-war. A fly is none of those: it is timing, reading and restraint — and now it is its own way to fish.*

- **The Fly Rod** — bamboo, string and an iron nugget; takes a small reel (1000–2000) as its drag and a built-in **Fly Rig**: a leader for the tippet and a slot that takes only a fly you tied on the Tackle Station's Tie page. The same tied lure is a mormyshka through the ice and a fly here.
- **The cast is a rhythm.** Hold use and a needle sweeps a bar; the two ends are the two stops of a fly cast. Tap sneak on each end for a false cast and a block more line; release on a beat for a tight loop. Off the beat is a splash that spooks the water; well past the end is a wind knot and line wear. Distance is aim.
- **The drift.** Twelve seconds; on flowing water the fly goes with the current and the line bows and drags — the tension slot shows it, and a dragging fly is refused. **Left-click mends.** A third mend in one drift spooks; a line straight below you catches nothing, so pick up and cast again.
- **The rise.** The fish is drawn coming up under the fly, nose first, with a ring on the water — and **the strike bar is a clock, not a sweep**: the marker climbs once across the window and the green is the species' own delay. Lift before it and you pulled the fly out of its mouth; after it and the fish spat it. Every miss puts the fish down for twenty seconds.
- **The hatch.** Midges in winter, stoneflies on spring days, mayflies at dusk, caddis on summer evenings, ants on a clear summer afternoon, scuds in still water, fry in autumn and under a storm. When a hatch is on, the water shows it — motes over the surface and rise rings from fish that are not yours. **Match it:** the right kind of fly within 3 mm is ×1.5, the right kind at the wrong size ×1.0, the wrong kind ×0.6. With nothing hatching a dry fly is ×0.7, a streamer ×0.9 and a nymph ×1.0 — nymphs catch most fish most of the time.
- The backcast needs no room behind you yet, and there is no roll cast: those come with the second phase, with the strip-set and the indicator.

## Species

### 267 species, from one table
The species live in a spreadsheet now (`tools/import_species_xlsx.py` reads it): 160 new fish join the 107 — the North American sunfishes, basses, crappies, gars, catfishes and sturgeons, the African cichlids, bichirs, lungfishes and tigerfish, the Asian mahseers, barbels, tilapias and snakeheads, the South American stingrays, characins and knifefishes, and 30 hybrids. Every old species got its description and parameters reviewed.

- **Six new families**: panfish, catfishes, cichlids, characins, rays and exotics, filed in the journal beside the old ones.
- **Diet**: every species is a predator, an omnivore, peaceful or an insect feeder. The journal shows it; the fly a species takes is chosen by it.
- **Afrotropical**, the fifth faunal province — carved out of a third of the Neotropic and Indomalaya cells, so an existing world keeps its Palearctic and Nearctic exactly where they were.
- **Hybrids breed**: a cross the table names (tiger muskie, splake, saugeye, wiper, bester, the tilapia and sunfish crosses…) spawns that hybrid in the tank instead of the mother's species.

### What a species no longer asks for
The ideal rod, rig, reel size and reel tolerance, the cast distance band and the hook tolerance are gone from the profiles and from the bite engine. A species asks for bait, feed, line and hook size; how you deliver them is your business. The match is bait 45 %, groundbait 20 %, line 20 %, hook 15 %.

### Angler level
The ladder runs **0–50** now (the old 0–12 stretched, the new species placed beside their nearest old neighbours). The level never forbids a fish: short of the rung, the bite thins in proportion down to a floor of 15 %.

### The fight clock
The fight timeout's ceiling is **6000 ticks** (five minutes), up from 3400, and a fish's run count now scales with its size: the profile's runs belong to the full-grown fish, a specimen well under its species' top weight makes fewer of them (40 % at the bottom of the range). The giants — the white and kaluga sturgeons, the Mekong and redtail catfish, the river stingrays — keep every run their profile gives them, and a 20 kg juvenile no longer out-dives its own clock.

### The shoal reads the water's depth

The fish you see under the surface were gated on the depth of one column at the edge of each patch, a bank column more often than not, so a species that wants three blocks of water was missing from a lake that has six a step away. The shoal now sits on the deepest of its probe columns and asks the species gate about the deepest water within three blocks, the same question the finder asks.

### The hook has a mouth, and the fish its own strength

A #10 was taking one-gram catfish and three-gram clarias, because the hook-size gradient lets a hook eight sizes off through. Now the smallest hook on the rig names the smallest fish that can take it (40 g at #8, halving every two sizes down, doubling every two up): a species whose biggest specimen is smaller does not take, and every other species' weight roll is floored there, the same rule a lure's mass already applied.

A fish's pull follows the specimen too. The profile's strength belongs to a mean fish, and a 72 g barbel used to pull like the two-kilo fish the table describes. Strength now scales with the square root of weight over the species' mean, floored at 0.35.

The sea species are back to no provinces at all. The oceans carry a cod everywhere, and an old world keeps every fish it had.

### The tune-up

- **Small fish fight small.** The pattern's extra runs (the amur's +3, the tuna's +3) scale with the specimen now, as the profile's own runs already did. A 72 g barbel is a two-run fight, not a five-run one.
- **Three rods, three bells.** The long rod's random spread is 0–60 s (was 0–45), the ice clamp 160 s (was 120), and a dead water coming back to life re-clocks every waiting line with its own phase.
- **Bottom Sense**, a seventh perk: −8 %/rank snags, a dead snag rarer again, −5 %/rank breaks under strain. The hardcore preset's snag multiplier is 1.3 (was 1.6).
- **Line repair.** Four string around a worn spool of line gives it back fresh.
- **Tackle comes from the stall.** The crafting recipes of what the fisherman sells assembled, or the bench ties, are gone: ten rods, nine reels, twelve lines, eleven lures, six rigs, two leaders. Whatever nobody sells keeps its recipe.
- **The sounder reads farther.** The section view covers 36 m (was 23) and shows up to 16 fish (was 10).
- **The fish that out-pulls the line says so.** The hook-up names the ratio and the rule (open the drag in a run, rod across, let it tire), and says it again as the first run begins. The played-out fight lands faster: the open-drag landing rate is 0.9 of the fatigue rate, was 0.55.
- **Hybrids are the tank's fish.** In wild water a hybrid weighs one twenty-fifth of its profile — the sunfish crosses stop out-biting the sunfish; stocked and settled it fishes like anything else. The catch card names the cross.
- **Tooltips.** A line names its type instead of a lang key; a catch card names the family in the journal's words for every group.

### Quests and achievements

The quest chain used to ask for roach, crucian, bream, rudd, tench, perch, pike, zander, asp, carp, trout, sterlet and grayling — a Palearctic pond. An angler who spawned in the Afrotropical could not finish stage 1. Stages 1–7 now ask for what the water anywhere holds: a peaceful feeder, an omnivore, a predator, a family, a kilo, five kilos, a salmonid, a fish on the fly. The soil reward is gone. ### Fly fishing, rebuilt

The phase-one cast wanted a tap on every end of a fast needle and a release on "the lit end"; the drift was twelve seconds of a drag number. Now:

- **The fish show themselves.** Hold a fly rod near water and feeding fish rise in front of you — a ring, a splash, a sip — a few at a time, each holding its lie for ten seconds. Put the fly within 2.5 blocks of a rise and you are *on the fish*: the take comes in a second or two, and it is that fish.
- **The cast is a hold and one release.** Hold use and the rod false-casts on its own, two metres more in the air on every stop. Release in the green (the forward stop) for a tight loop; a little early opens it and lands short; with the rod behind you it piles. A left-click on a stop hauls two metres more. Nothing else can go wrong.
- **The drift reads itself.** One line under the crosshair: *dead drift*, *dragging — left-click to mend*, *line's straight — hold right-click*, *on the fish — wait*. Tap right-click to strip (streamers and shrimps are fished by it, a swirl behind the fly says a fish is following), hold right-click to pick the line up and go straight into the next cast.
- **The strip comes in like a retrieve.** The fly's spot stays where the cast put it and every strip brings the line a metre's worth toward you on the same smooth path the spinning lure takes; the fight starts from where the fly was.
- **The take hooks itself.** No strike bar on the fly rod: the fish turns on the hook and you are straight into the fight.
- **The set is the show.** On a fly rod the hook-up throws the fish out of the water in a burst of spray, with a slap the whole bank hears — and everyone sees the fish for that moment.
- **One rhythm, not two.** The winter rod's jig follows the cast's rule: hold right-click and the rod jigs on its own, every stop pulling the bite a little closer; a left-click on a stop is an accent (the combo, a bigger pull); letting go is the pause. No stroke can be jerked by clicking any more.
- **Reels up to 6000** fit the fly rod now (was 2000): the salmon and the sea fish want a real drag.
