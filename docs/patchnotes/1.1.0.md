# River Fishing 1.1.0 — Patch Notes (draft, in progress)

**Minecraft 1.21.1** (Fabric & NeoForge) · **26.2** (NeoForge) · **26.3** (Fabric & NeoForge) · 1.20.1 to follow · **26.1.2 is no longer supported**

Everything here is compared to **1.0.0**, the last released version. This file is kept up to date while 1.1.0
is being built; nothing in it is released yet.

## Welcome to 1.1.0

Hey anglers.

Until now a fish only existed for the second your cast asked about it. That is over. In 1.1.0 every body
of water is alive: shoals get hungry, move with the hour and the season, eat, learn your baits, spawn,
grow old and get eaten, whether you are there or not. Your job is to read that water and find the way
in. On top of it come boilies — paste, flavours, pop-ups and dips, judged by real carp-angling rules —
and a journal and a fish finder rebuilt from scratch.

---

## Patch Highlights

* **33 new species**, 295 in all: from the common minnow and the taran to the Russian sturgeon, the opah and the
  Greenland shark.
* **Living water.** Every lake, river and sea region holds real shoals and single trophy fish that live
  on their own, and the bite comes from them. Prebait a swim for a week and the carp learn it; hammer a
  shoal and it really thins out, then recovers.
* **Private ponds rebuilt.** Every fish in a pond is a record of its own. Release a trophy or a legend and
  catch that same fish again. Real spawning: roe, fry, young fish. A pond's size decides how many fish it
  holds, and it grows only on what you feed it. Old ponds keep their fish.
* **The wild balances itself.** Old age, starvation, predators and anglers take fish; only spawning brings
  new ones. Checked over a thousand game days.
* **Boilies.** Boil your own from paste in a cauldron over a campfire: 14 flavours, sinker / wafter /
  pop-up / snowman, 10-24 mm, dips, prebaiting. Which flavour works depends on the season, the hour, the
  sky, the pressure, the water, the bottom and the fish.
* **The journal, redesigned.** Four bookmarks instead of eight tabs, a species page in three sheets, a new
  leather-bound look and smooth animations.
* **The fish finder is an instrument now**, and the corner sonar strip shows the fish that are actually
  there.
* **A cast is never refused.** Cast wherever you like; an empty puddle simply stays empty.
* **New plants and snags.** Corn grows two blocks tall; barley and peas are redrawn. Reeds and cattails grow
  wild by the water, villages plant the bait crops, and snags lie on river beds as cover for fish.

## Living Water

* Every water body is split into zones (roughly a chunk each) with their own depth, bottom, weed and food.
  Up to six species per zone, drawn by weight, so rare fish still turn up.
* Shoals are groups with a count and an average weight; big fish can be single trophy fish with their own
  memory.
* Fish have hunger, wariness and memory. They move to where the food, the cover and the hour suit them:
  carp in the shallows at dawn, dusk and night, deep at a hot summer noon; predators follow their prey.
* Water you are not at is not simulated every tick — it catches up when you come back (up to three days
  hour by hour, longer gaps in one step), so it costs almost nothing.
* **Groundbait moves fish.** A fed swim pulls shoals from the zones around it. A shoal that has eaten its
  fill bites less — **overfeeding can now hurt**.
* **Fish learn baits.** A week of prebaiting and a shoal keeps coming back to that bait after you stop.
* **Catches are real.** A landed fish is gone from its shoal; a lost or released fish makes its shoal
  warier for a couple of days. A released trophy is shy, then forgets.
* **Why no bite** is read off the fish themselves: no fish near the bait (and where the nearest are),
  fish there but refusing your rig (and why), or what is holding the keenest one back — full, season,
  hour, weather, wary, spooked.
* The hint lines can be switched off for immersion: `/rffish hints off` (and `on`), per player.
* Ice holes still use the old engine; they will be rebuilt separately.

## Casting and Predators

* **A cast is never refused.** If nothing near wants your rig, the line simply waits; it comes alive when
  the water changes. The hint still tells you why.
* Predators strike on reflex: a pike that has just fed still takes a lure.
* A lure worked back through the water reaches fish about 20 blocks off; a still bait about 12.

## The Wild Balances Itself

* Fish die of old age (a lifespan from 4 to 40 game years, by species size), of starvation, to predators
  and to anglers. New fish come only from spawning.
* Shoals spawn on their spawning grounds in their season — shallow water with weed or mud — and you can
  see it happen.
* Predators pick the commonest prey, so a rare species gets a refuge; small predators also graze.
* A shoal fished down to almost nothing gets strays from nearby water every ten days or so — a water
  cannot be fished dead, but it can be fished thin.
* Over a thousand simulated days an unfished pond holds steady with all its species; a pond fished two
  hours every dawn drops to about a third and recovers within 3-4 years once you stop.

## Private Ponds

* The old stocking system is replaced. **Every fish in a pond is a record**: sex, weight, age, genes, card,
  morph, name.
* **Release a trophy or a legend and catch the same fish again.** The net keeps its badge. A re-caught
  legend gives no second achievement.
* **Real spawning.** A mature female with a mature male in the species' spawning window lays roe; roe
  hatches into fry in three days; fry grow into young fish in about 16 days. Genes are inherited. Weed hides
  fry, predators eat them.
* **Room by water volume.** Roughly a quarter of a fish and 0.4 kg per water block (+50% with full cover).
  A puddle never holds a thousand fish. Snags add cover; feeding stations and aerators add richness.
* **Ponds grow only on feed.** An unfed pond barely grows. No fish of yours ever dies of old age or crowding
  in your pond — crowding stops growth and starves fry instead.
* **Old ponds are carried over:** every fish on the old book becomes a fish of the living pond.
* Fixed: the "phantom fish" in ponds — releasing one male and netting four, or never being able to empty
  a pond.
* Fixed: spawning time came and no roe was laid.
* **A hopper feeds the feeding station:** set one on top and it drops groundbait jars in, one at a time, until the
  station is full.
* Fixed: fry never grew up if you slept through the nights. A pond aged only while the game actually ran, but its
  spawning followed the calendar, so a night slept through or a `/time add` brought the spawning season on time
  while the fry never reached their 16 days. Living water now keeps time with the calendar; setting the clock back
  no longer stops it.
* **The net grades like the rod:** a fish netted out of your own pond is a trophy by its weight and prime by its
  weight, and a prime one sells to the fisherman and fills the order of the day. A poached fish is never prime.
* **Every grown fish shows its sex.** Fish grown from fry now count as females and males on the finder from the
  day they grow up, not only once they mature, and the pond sign lists the young apart: "12 grown, 30 young, 5 fry".

## Releasing Into the Wild

* A fish you release into wild water becomes a real fish of that water, with its card and badges. A
  released trophy stays catchable as itself; a released pair spawns there.
* Your own released fish can be netted back legally; netting anything else is still poaching.
* Old wild stocking records are carried over into the living water once.

## Underwater

* The fish you see are the living water's fish, and there are many more of them: up to 320 around you, and
  up to 700 when you dive — a wall of fish in a good swim.
* Fish behave by role: bottom fish creep along the bed, pike hang still and dash, small shoalers school
  tight, other predators patrol. Resting fish sink and slow down.
* You can see feeding (bubbles and silt over a groundbait spot), rising (rings at dawn and dusk), hunting
  (a dash, a splash, small fish scattering) and spawning (splashing in the weed).
* A shoal that moves zone swims over instead of blinking.
* Jumps, spook flight, schooling and predators eyeing the bait all stay.
* Fewer splash sounds.
* Fixed: koi drawn plain white; every koi and carp now shows its real variety and pattern.

## Boilies

* **Boilie paste:** 1-4 wheat (size 10 / 15 / 20 / 24 mm) + an egg + up to two flavours + up to two dried
  kelp (one makes a wafter, two a pop-up) + optional fish meal.
* **Boiling:** right-click a water cauldron over a lit campfire with the paste — four boilies per paste.
* **Flavours (14):** strawberry, tutti-frutti, plum, pineapple, citrus, caramel, chocolate, sweetcorn, fish,
  krill, squid, liver, garlic and chili. A bottle is a glass bottle + sugar + the ingredient (sweet berries,
  melon, chorus fruit, glow berries, apple, honey, cocoa, corn, fish oil, prismarine crystals, ink sac,
  chicken liver, allium, blaze powder). The fisherman sells some at level 3.
* **Snowman:** a sinker or wafter + a pop-up.
* **Dip:** carry a flavour bottle onto a stack of boilies and right-click. It lasts eight casts and makes
  the smell carry further in murky water.
* **Two flavours, two colours:** a boilie is drawn half in the colour of each flavour, and a dipped one glistens,
  with a drop of the dip running off it.
* **Prebaiting:** right-click water with boilies to throw ten in. The fish that eat them learn the
  flavour.
* **The rules** (a wide corridor — the right boilie is worth about six of the wrong one): cold water wants
  protein and spice, warm water fruit, sweet and sour; overcast and rain want heavy smells; a hot clear
  noon wants light sweet and sour and puts fish off meat; a pressure jump wakes fish only for a sour bright
  pop-up; murky water wants strong smells and dips, clear water natural ones (big fish most of all); mud
  swallows mild smells, a clean bottom suits fish and seafood; bright colours go with fruit, dark with
  protein. Dawn, day, evening and night each have their own preferences. Predators want meat and fish.
  Small fish strip small sweet boilies; 20 mm and up is too big for them.
* The journal remembers each species' **favourite flavour** once three fish have come out on it.
* **The fisherman rolls his own boilies:** a journeyman sells a single-flavour bottom boilie, wafter or
  small pop-up, an expert two-flavour ones, snowmen, fish-meal bases and dipped boilies — each stall its
  own. The three flavour bottles share one trade slot.

* **Gear** has seven pages: rods, reels, lines, rigs, baits, lures and **boilies**. The boilie page has three

* Smooth animations: the book lifts in, bookmarks slide out, the open tab slides, pages turn, scrolling

* A scan line sweeps the screen when you switch it on or change the view.

## The Fish Card

* **A new card, the mod's own.** No more badges over a column of "Label: value" rows — a fish shows as a
  catch record:
  * its **weight, big**, pounds beside it;
  * a **scale** of its own kind: the bar runs from the lightest catches to the heaviest *by how often they
    come*, so the marker's place is exactly "heavier than N % of its kind" — the same curve the game rolls
    weights on — and a gold tick marks where a trophy starts ("trophy from 5.5 kg");
  * its **length on a measuring board**, the fish laid nose to the stop;
  * the length in **inches** beside the centimetres;
  * sex, size and nature on one line, with its variety or morph;
  * **stamps** tilted over the corner — TROPHY, LEGEND, SPECIAL, PRIME, FOUL-HOOKED, NET, POACHED;
  * the **rod and the bait as their own pictures**, where it came from, who and when; a prime fish shows its
    price in emeralds.
  * Shift shows the conditions (time, season, weather, bottom) and the pedigree (group, ecosystem, hybrid,
    pattern, genes).
* The card sits on a dark "deep water" ground, the fish's standing only in its corners.
* Where a fish came from reads as its **region and the water** ("Palearctic · river"), not the biome and the water
  ("River · river").
* **A contract is a paper order form**: the terms written in ink on dotted lines, a box for each fish
  (ticked as they go into the bag), the pay as emeralds and experience, a stamp that says ORDER or EXPIRED.

## Competitions

* **`/rffish spawn <species> <count> [grams]`** puts fish into the living water where the command runs — as
  shoals the species keeps (a big lone predator in threes at most), each in its own zone, nearest first.
  **`/rffish spawn <species> trophy [grams]`** puts in one trophy fish. They are ordinary fish of that water:
  they feed, move, bite and are caught like its own.
* **`/rffish clear`** takes the event's fish out again and leaves the water's own; **`/rffish clear all`**
  empties the water entirely (it refills slowly on its own). **`/rffish census`** lists what swims there,
  species by species, and how much of it is the event's.
* For operators (permission level 2); they work from the console and command blocks with
  `execute positioned`. Private ponds are left to their owners.
* **The catch card keeps the minute the fish came out**, shown beside the angler in your own clock (fish from
  before 1.1.0 keep only the date). The tournament server mod reads it, so a fish caught before an event starts
  doesn't count.

* **A new window**, larger and drawn as a cabinet in the Tackle Station's style: tank glass in a brass rim,

## Plants

* **Snags lie in the water**: small clusters on the bed of rivers, swamps and lakes out in the open — never in a
  forest, a desert or the sea (nor within 24 blocks of it), and never where the water is less than three blocks deep. The snag pile has five looks now —
  the old tangle, a half-sunk log, an upturned root stump, a forked branch and a heap of bleached driftwood — picked at
  random wherever one stands, placed by hand or grown by the world. A wild snag is cover like one you set: predators
  hold by it and fry hide in it, so a river snag is a spot worth casting at. A pond sign lists the snags in its water too.
* **Reed** and **Cattail**, two new bank plants two blocks tall, to dress up your swim: the reed with cream
  plumes, the cattail with brown heads. They grow wild along rivers, lakes and swamps, stand on soil, sand,
  gravel or clay, and are gathered with shears.
* **Villages grow the bait crops**: farms and field plots plant corn, peas and barley among the wheat,
  carrots and potatoes — corn and peas in the savanna, barley and peas in the taiga and snow, all three on the plains.
* **Corn grows two blocks tall**, in five stages instead of four: it sprouts, grows knee-high, shoots past your
  head and ripens with a golden tassel and a cob. It needs a free block above it to grow past the third stage.
  Corn planted before the update keeps growing; a plant that was already ripe needs one more stage.
* **Barley is drawn in 3D**: blades turning from green to gold as it ripens, and heavy golden ears bending over at the
  top, over its four stages.
* **Peas are redrawn**: a sprout, a young vine, white flowers, then pods hanging among the leaves.
* **Reeds and cattails stand in shallow water:** in water one block deep they take root with their feet wet. If
  water reaches the top half (two blocks deep, or water poured over it) the plant breaks. Only shears gather them.
* **No more seeds for the bait crops.** Corn is planted from corn, peas from peas, barley from pearl barley. Old
  seed items turn into these in your inventory. Grass no longer drops them, so the crops come from villages, and
  the fisherman sells corn, peas and pearl barley instead of seeds.
* **Ripe corn gives corn cobs** and **ripe peas give pea pods:** eat one (2.5 food) or craft it into four corn or
  four peas. An unripe plant gives back what was planted.
* **Ripe barley gives wheat as well as pearl barley**, the way wheat gives wheat and seeds.

## The Fight

Every fight pattern now has a move of its own, at most twice a fight, and each is answered with something the fight already
asks for — the rod held across, the drag opened, the reel:

* **Zig-zag** (burst): one run that switches side two or three times; answer each leg like a run.
* **Into the weeds** (steady): a heavy run for cover. Hold the rod across and you turn it; let it go and it is in the
  weeds — nothing comes on the reel until you open the drag and it swims out by itself.
* **Torpedo** (relentless): a long run straight away. A closed drag loads toward the break; an open one lets it take
  line, and the fish is spent for it.
* **Sulk** (sounding): after a dive it lies on the bottom and the reel does nothing. Lift the rod and it comes up.
* **Charge** (aggressive): it turns and comes straight at you. Reel, or the line goes slack and it can throw the hook.
* **Tail-walk** (greyhounding): three jumps one after another, each answered like any jump.
* **Plank** (active then passive): beaten, it lies on its side and is towed in.

The cue under the boss bar names the answer for each (open the drag, lift, reel fast).

At the bank: a tired fish rolls onto its side on the surface, and a landed fish is lifted out of the water to your hands,
kicking and dripping. One that gets away turns from the rod and goes down. A fight that runs out of time ends with the
hook pulling out.

The fish is where the line says: a run that takes line carries it out, and a side run swings it round you on the tight
line. Splashes, weed and bubbles come up where the fish is.

**Play at the bottom.** The fish shows up where it took the bait: deep under a float set to the bottom, on the bed
for a bottom rig, just under the surface for a fly. It fights down there, near the bottom, and comes up only as it
tires and nears the bank. Dives and sulks go down to the bed of the swim, never through it.

**Into the weeds needs weeds.** A steady fish runs for cover only when there is cover near it: weed, kelp, lilies,
reeds, cattails or snags. In open water, like the sea off a beach, it just runs. The cue says what it went into (the
weeds, the reeds or the snags), and the bits that fly up are that cover's own.

## The Rod and the Line Come Alive

Every action with the rod now has a bit of anticipation, a snap and a follow-through that overshoots and settles.

* **Picking the rod up:** it swings up into your hands and the tip whips once. Grab a rod off the pod on a bite
  and it comes up already bent into the fish.
* **The reel turns when you wind it:** each click is a turn of the handle. On a run the handle stops, ticks
  backward and chatters while the drag gives line.
* **The retrieve:** the tip goes down to the water and sways with the handle. Every click flicks it and darts the
  lure toward you; pause, and the line bellies.
* **Strikes and takes:** a hookset sweeps the rod up. A take on a lure or a bottom rig slams the tip down and kicks
  the line aside.
* **The fight:** the tip beats with the fish's tail and head shakes knock it. A jump slackens the line and the
  landing yanks it tight. The arrow keys hold the rod over, and near the bank you raise it high.
* **The line in the water:** a running fish cuts it through the surface with a hiss of spray and a trail of
  bubbles, and a line that snaps tight throws off drops.
* **When the fish is gone:** a snapped line leaves a limp piece on the tip that falls and curls away; a thrown hook
  whips the whole line back through the air. Either way the rod springs straight and shakes out.
* **Walking:** the tip bounces with your steps, a landing whips it, and sprinting carries the rod high.
* **On the rod pod:** the rod bends now. It keeps a slight bow on a tight line, taps on the nibbles before a take,
  nods in jerks on a take, and on a self-hooked fish it goes right over and rattles in its rests. The line pulls
  in time with it. A bell swings, a digital alarm lights up with every beep, and a false alarm only shivers the line.
* `/rfrod anim` shows the state; `off` switches the new motion off, `flip` turns the rod's up and down over if it
  ever moves the wrong way.

## Fixes

* Dry farmland under corn, peas or barley no longer turns back to dirt and drops the crop; it holds, as under wheat.
* A beaten fish no longer flips upright and back onto its side at every twitch of the line, a flatfish no longer
  stands on its edge when beaten, and a fish being towed in stays in the water instead of floating up toward the
  angler's feet.
* **A young fish is measured by its weight.** A fish lighter than its species' adults (one grown up from fry in a
  pond) had the adults' shortest length: a 3 kg mako was 150 cm. Its length now shrinks with its weight (that mako is
  about 80 cm), and its icon shrinks with it, down to a much smaller size than before.
* **The rod no longer dips down and up on every click.** Each turn of the reel and each twitch of a lure made
  Minecraft replay its "item used" bob, and the server's bend written onto the rod replayed the item-swap bob too.
  Both are off for rods; the rod's own motion shows the click now.
* **Rod pod: take the rod you aim at.** An empty hand takes the rod nearest to where you click, and the one it would
  take lifts a little as you look at the pod. A rod with a fish on it still comes first. A rod is docked on the empty
  rest you click by, and an alarm goes on the rod you click by.
* Rod pod: taking the first rod no longer moves the line of the second one. The pod numbered its rods by counting the
  ones it drew, so the rest were renumbered and their lines lost their tips.
* A running fish taking line on an open drag now really swims farther out; before, only the boss bar showed it going
  while the fish stayed at the reeled-in distance.
* **Shaders no longer send the line flying.** The rod measures once which space the hand is drawn in and
  saved that answer for good; switching a shaderpack on afterwards moved the hand into the other space and
  the line swung about until `/rfrod handspace auto`. It now keeps checking on every clear turn of the head and
  follows the change by itself; only `/rfrod handspace view|world` pins it.
* A rod pod's fish is a fish of the living water, like a held rod's: pod catches thin a shoal and take a
  pond's own fish, instead of copies.
* A fish two anglers (or a rod and a net) are after comes ashore once; the second gets an ordinary fish of
  its kind, never a second copy of a named or legendary one.
* Old mirror, linear, leather and koi fish released into a pond come back out as themselves.
* Fish released under the old stocking are carried into the overworld's living water only, not copied into
  every dimension.
* Pond upgrades (snags, aerator, feeding station) now count in ponds above or below sea level.
* A pond visited after a long absence no longer stalls the server with a very long season length.
* Wild fish no longer move about faster the more players watch the water.
* On a server, a bait's journal page lists the fish that take it again (it came up empty off single player).
* **A rare crash near rivers with Embeddium or Sodium is fixed** (MissingPaletteEntryException after hours of play). The
  river current was read from the world while chunks were still loading; a read that catches the world mid-load
  now draws that one block still until it is redrawn.
* A bigger keepnet or tackle box crafted around a smaller one keeps its contents, dye and name; it used to
  come out empty.
* Emeralds are paid in stacks of 64: a big sale no longer drops one oversized stack that could break a save.
* A bait trap in someone else's pond no longer lifts that pond's fry.
* The order of the day pays ×2.5 on top of the market price, so a flooded market lowers it like any other fish.
* The order of the day can be filled on the world's very first day.
* Setting the time back no longer freezes market recovery and the feeding station's decay.
* Groundbait spots that have gone cold are forgotten instead of piling up in the save.
* A dye on a plain groundbait jar colours it, instead of being used up for nothing.
* A worm farm, maggot farm or bait trap drops what is in it when broken.
* A cooked fish can no longer be put in the mini-aquarium.
* **Two item duplications closed.** A tackle box set down as a block can be open for one player at a time
  (two at once printed whatever one of them took out). The rod assembly screen, a held tackle box and a rig
  now belong to the exact item they were opened on: swapping another rod, box or rig into the hand with the
  number keys closes the screen instead of copying the tackle onto it.
* No cast can start while a screen is open.
* Only the rod whose line is out goes on a rod pod; any bottom rod used to take the line, so one baited rig
  could fill a pod with bare rods that never used bait.
* Grabbing a biting pod rod in the middle of another fight ends that fight properly (its boss bar stayed on
  screen until relog).
* A fishing session ends when the angler changes dimension or dies.
* The fly is only fished on a fly rod, and its bite clock runs on game time, however often the client
  reports.
* The Tackle Station no longer slows down the more often it has been opened; shift-clicking its result keeps
  the next one ready; materials put into a bench broken while its screen was open are no longer lost.
* A fly tied in several colours pays for the thread of every colour.
* A `season_days` setting that does not divide by three no longer crashes the finder and the aquarium.
* Old, unused code, textures and translation lines from before 1.0 are removed.
* The living water's save no longer grows without end or rewrites itself on every autosave. It is written only
  when some water has changed, keeps each species name once, and forgets wild water nobody has fished for a
  month — that water grows back from the world the way unvisited water always has. Ponds, fish put back and
  event fish are always kept. Young born in wild water get a record of their own only while it holds fewer
  than 256 (a released stock's first years, where its young grow into big fish one by one); past that they grow
  up as ordinary fish of that water (a fish put back always keeps its record).
* **The Warm Outflow works in open water and ponds:** in winter, the fish by it bite as if spring had already
  come, which makes a winter hotspot you can build. Before, it only showed up on the finder.

* Fish can be shift-clicked into a furnace or smoker on 26.x (before, only dragging worked).
* Releasing a fish into a pond no longer shows a raw translation key.
* Rod pods keep a line that has no fish assigned yet.
* Fly rods named the wrong reels: the tooltip offered a spinning reel size window (1000-6000) instead of the fly reel of the rod's own class. A fly rod now says "Takes reel: Fly Reel #5", a fly reel says which rod it is for, the tackle bench explains a wrong fly reel, and the journal's gear table shows the class.

**From the 26.3 beta testers:**

* **The reel is on the rod.** On 26.x it was never drawn, in the hand, in third person or on a rod pod: the rod
  looked its reel up under the wrong name and quietly drew nothing.
* **A trophy is a trophy by its weight.** The badge was read off the roll, and the size genes and a pond's
  average then moved the fish, so a 13.43 kg carp could be a trophy under a card saying "trophy from 13.60 kg".
* The stamps on the catch card stand level: tilted, the pixel letters ran into each other.
* **The rod follows the fish.** On a side run the rod leaned and bent the moment the run began, while the fish
  took seconds to swing round. The lean and the bend now go with the fish as it is drawn.
* The Tie tab's size reading sits under the wells beside the hook number; on the readout line it ran into the
  pattern's name at every GUI scale.
* The finder's bed legend has its own strip at the foot of the section (it printed over the deepest depth mark
  and over the sand), and the species list keeps "…and N more below" inside the screen.
* **The order of the day shows on small screens.** When the contract board has no room beside the trading
  window it hangs under the order sign; it used to drop to the same place and cover it.
* The aquarium water takes the world's light. At night it glowed like a block of ice and tinted the sky seen
  through the tank.
* The fisherman's trusted shelf gives the villager 2 experience a sale, not 20: ten 6-emerald bite alarms took a
  novice stall halfway through Expert. A shelf offer already on a counter is swapped the next time it opens.
