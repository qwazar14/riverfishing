# River Fishing 0.8.3 — the fish finder became an instrument, and the fish became fish

---

**Minecraft 1.20.1** (Fabric & Forge) · **1.21.1** (Fabric & NeoForge) · **26.1.2** (Fabric & NeoForge)
· **26.2** (Fabric & NeoForge).

This one is mostly Gabbi's list, plus a crash three people sent in, plus a cave lake. The fish finder
is a screen now, with a chart you build over the game; the journal draws recipes instead of naming
them; there are contracts; and the fish in the water are the fish that bite, with bodies.

## The crash on casting a wooden rod

Reported by Guest666_, freddiedredd98 and kalibs, all on NeoForge 1.21.1, all the same words: *cast the
stick rod, the game dies*. The crash report, when it came, was ours line for line:

```
java.lang.IllegalStateException: Not building!
    at com.mojang.blaze3d.vertex.BufferBuilder.ensureBuilding
    at LineRenderer.drawFloat(LineRenderer.java:207)
```

The line renderer took the `lines()` buffer once and handed it down. Further in it asked the same
buffer source for the line-strand type — and asking ends the batch of the type handed out last — so the
first consumer was already dead when the bobber was drawn into it. Fifty lines and one call apart,
which is why it survived review.

Only float rigs draw a bobber, and the float class is exactly the four reel-less rods: stick, bamboo,
pole, winter. So it read as *"the stick rod kills the game"* when it was **any float rod, every cast**.
The rod pod had the same shape waiting — one cached buffer, a per-rod strand fetch in the loop. Both now
ask for the buffer where they draw into it, which is the only version of this that cannot rot.

## A cast could land in a cave

*"If there is a lake underground, the line goes there while you stand on the surface."* It did: the
column scan walked twenty-four blocks straight down from above your head and took the first water it
met, through dirt, stone and floors alike. The cast target is yaw and distance only — pitch is not
read — so there was no way to aim at that water and no way to see it.

The tackle falls to the water, so the scan now stops at the first block that would stop it falling.
Ice is the one thing it must fall through: the ice check sits just below and is what says *drill a
hole*. Known cost: water under a roof can no longer be cast into from outside the roof — stand under it
and it works, because the scan starts under it too.

## The rod pod handed you the wrong rod

Gabbi, three rods on a pod: *"right-click should take the one a fish bit, not an empty one."* The
preference for a bitten rod was there from the start; it just could not tell a fish from a false alarm.
A phantom bite sets the same flag for a shorter window, so an alarm that had cried wolf on rod one
outranked the fish actually hanging on rod three. One term — `!phantom` — and the pod hands over the
right rod.

## The journal draws instead of naming

*"Too much text. I'd put in pictures of crafts and catches instead."*

**Recipes are a bench now.** The slots where the recipe puts them, an arrow, the result, tooltips on
hover; a tag ingredient steps through everything it accepts once a second, the way the recipe book does.
The catch list on a bait's page is the fish themselves. Nothing is hand-written per item — one scan
behind the grid reads whatever the data pack defines, so a recipe changed in JSON changes the page.

**The guide pages have sections.** They always had them — a section opened as an ALL-CAPS phrase inside
the paragraph because there was nothing else it could do. A paragraph written as `## Something` in the
lang file is a heading now, and the six longest pages (26 headings) were promoted with **not one word
changed**. The languages move together: a heading that appears in Russian and not in English is a hard
error in the wiki check, and "ПКМ" is an acronym, so it always looks like a heading where "Right-click"
does not. The tool intersects the candidates across all three and takes only what every language agrees
on.

## Contracts

The first thing in the mod that **takes the fish**. The order of the day pays you for catching one, so
the fish is still in the bag afterwards and still gets sold — there was never a choice. A contract:
*bring three bream over a kilo, be paid for the set, and the bream are gone.*

Three stand at a time, at the top of the journal's Quests tab. A row lights up when the fish are in
your bag or in a keepnet in it — counted on the client from its own inventory, counted again on the
server before anything is taken. It takes the **smallest** qualifying fish first (a contract must never
walk off with the trophy when an ordinary fish would do), and takes nothing at all unless the whole set
is there. Nothing about the board is stored: which three stand today is derived from the world day and
who is asking, so it is the same board all day, survives a restart and costs no save data. Drawn from
species you have already caught — a contract naming a fish you have never seen is a wiki lookup.

The first test build asked for *a peacock bass from 1753 kilograms*. The profile field is `weight_g`,
grams already, and the code multiplied by a thousand on the way in. Nothing complained — 1 753 100 is a
perfectly good integer — and the cap it was clamped against carried the same ×1000, so the two agreed
all the way to the screen. A player sent a picture. Bars are rounded now too: 50 g steps under a kilo,
100 g over. *From 251 g* is a computation showing its working; a job asks for 250.

## The fish finder is an instrument

*"A GUI for the finder — fish icons, water level, the fish. Chat is too much text."*

Everything it says was already computed and then printed as six lines of names. Right-click opens a
screen; hold it and a strip runs on the HUD.

**The section.** The left half is the water in section along the line you are aiming down — the real
bed, metre by metre, coloured by what it is made of (sand, gravel, clay, mud, rock), with the bank
where the water stops. A fish is drawn **where along that bed its depth is met**: a bream out where it
gets deep, a bleak under the surface, a pike-perch standing at the far end where the water finally
reaches it. Ten at most, at a size that reads — the textures are 256 px and twenty-eight of them at
16 px on one face were mush.

**Why a fish will not bite.** Clicking a blocked species names the gate — *wrong depth here*, *not
this biome*, *wrong season*. That diagnosis existed already and was shown to nobody: it was written for
the creative-only admin probe. It is the most useful sentence this tool has.

**The bottom moves the bite.** A nudge of 0.85–1.2, never a gate: cyprinids root in mud and clay,
predators ambush off rock and gravel, salmonids want gravel, sturgeon grub sand and mud, sea fish take
sand or rock and sulk over silt. A profile may say otherwise with a `"bed"` map; the ninety-three that
do not get their family's habit. Depth and water type already decide whether a fish *exists* here, and
a second hard gate stacked on them would empty half the swims over a bottom you cannot see without the
finder and cannot change at all.

**The strip.** While the finder is merely held, a sounder trace scrolls on the HUD — one column a
second, pushed on the right, the way a paper sounder wrote — so walking a bank draws the bed you walked
over. A HUD strip and not a screen, because a screen takes the controls, and a device you walk with
cannot be a screen. Under it, a needle to the nearest feature you have found, with its kind and distance;
pick a mark on the chart and the needle points at that one instead, measured from where you stand now.

**Sounding.** Crouch and right-click the water: a marker cast sweeps five blocks wide along your aim,
measures the bed under every block, and writes it down. It finds **holes** (deeper than the bed both
sides) and **drop-offs** (the bed steps away), and fish hold on both — casting on one is worth a real
bite bonus, paid the way a fed spot pays, so baiting a feature you found stacks. Features are read along
the cast's own line; a hole inferred from two casts that never crossed would be invented bed.

**The chart.** Everything you have ever sounded, on one face, to pan and zoom — the lake in front of
you and the one you measured last week — kept on your own disk per world, the way a minimap mod keeps
what you have seen. Six depth bands, a contour on every edge, the bank, a boat with a heading line,
a ring for a hole and a diamond for a drop-off. It has holes in it until somebody casts across them;
an honest chart of a lake nobody has sounded is empty, and filling it in is the game.

## The cast gauge reads in metres

It was a bar that filled: fifty percent, eighty-five, a colour. Nothing on it said how far the line
would go, and *how far* is the only thing an angler charging a cast wants to know. The reach is one
function now, read by the server when it lands the line and by the client when it draws the gauge, so
the metres over the bar are the metres the line lands at — by construction, not by two copies of the
same arithmetic agreeing until one is edited. The gauge itself is oak and brass with a lit tube for the
charge, the dead band an under-loaded rig cannot reach hatched red, ticks every five metres.

## The fish in the water

*"The rare biters are swimming around; the ones that bite often are almost never there."*

True, twice over. The shoal was weighted by how well the water **suits** a species and never by how
often it **takes** — a beluga at base 0.1 and a roach at 1.0 came up equally often. And a lone small
fish was not sent to a far cell because the client could not draw it big enough to see, which cut every
roach and bleak out of the lake past 26 blocks, leaving pike and sturgeon across the water.

The shoal is base × environment now, as the bite is. A species that takes once in ten stays rare on the
screen too — it passes through one hour in four, so *I saw a beluga* is an event. And small fish reach
the far bank as the **schools** they actually move in: you cannot make out one bleak from there, but
you can make out a shimmer of eight.

They behave like fish. A schooling species keeps its numbers — line up, close toward the middle, do not
sit on one another. A fish beats and glides rather than motoring, and the tail swings with the beat. A
predator that notices your bait goes to look and holds off it, curious for a few seconds and bored for a
dozen. Carp roll and salmon leap, on the fish's own clock, with the water's own splash.

**And they can have bodies — off by default.** No models, no entities: the third dimension is pulled
out of the sprite, the outline of the *body* given a flank each side and a thin back and belly, with
the fins left as the flat sprite. It did not pass the look test yet, so the shipped fish is the flat
swimming sprite; **`/rfrod fish3d on`** tries the bodies, persisted client-side, and `off` returns.

## Small things

- **A crank is not a swing.** Reeling made the rod flail like a sword — every crank returned the
  result that plays vanilla's arm swing, and the cast whip was added on top. Reeling returns `CONSUME`
  now; the cast still swings.
- **The compatibility page** existed for four days and was published to nobody — the wiki bundler
  carries a hardcoded page list and the safeguard that was meant to catch a page missing from it had
  never been written. Both fixed.
