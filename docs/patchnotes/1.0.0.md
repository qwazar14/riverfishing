# River Fishing 1.0.0 — Patch Notes

**Minecraft 1.20.1** (Fabric & Forge) · **1.21.1** (Fabric & NeoForge) · **26.1.2** (Fabric & NeoForge) · **26.2** (Fabric & NeoForge)

Everything below is measured against **0.9.0**, the last release. The 0.9.1 draft was folded into this note; nothing in it shipped on its own.

Hey anglers. This is the one where the mod stops apologising for its fly rod. The line is a rope now — a real one, with slack and pull, that floats or sinks, that the current carries and the bottom catches — and every fly cast, false cast, shoot, mend and strip comes out of moving that rope with the rod. Rivers run. The species table is the author's own, two hundred and sixty-seven fish. You tie your own flies on a bench that is twice the size. Every fish cooks. Your pond is yours in every way that matters, and it stops lying to you about what lives in it. And a beaten beluga can be brought the last metre.

It is a big one. Skim the highlights, then read the section you fish in.

---

## Patch Highlights

- **Fly fishing, rebuilt on a physical line** — a rope off the rod tip, three untimed inputs, a cast that is not scripted and only counts when the fly flew, nine lines, five rod classes, the fly fished the way its pattern is fished.
- **Rivers run** — river water has a current: the texture moves, boats and foam drift, the line bellies, a dragged dry fly cuts a wake.
- **267 species** (160 new) from the author's table — six new families, a diet per species, hybrids, and the fish are drawn where they should be: a stingray lies flat.
- **Every fish cooks** — in a furnace or a smoker, and comes out the same fish: same weight, same card. Prime fish give effects.
- **Your pond keeps its own book** — up to 100 × 100 of water, named, read from its sign, no gates on what you put in, and two ponds next to each other stop sharing fish.
- **The fight has a body** — the fish is on the line from the hook-up and visible the whole time; an out-pulled fish is played out, and a played-out fish comes to the crank.
- **Tie on a 32 × 32 canvas**, and a fly that matches no pattern rolls a hidden number.
- **Any fish is live bait** — and only a predator five times heavier will take it.
- **Two anglers on one bank hear their own drag** — the fight's sounds are the angler's own; a neighbour hears them quiet and low.

## Fly Fishing

*Five rebuilds inside 0.10 were deleted whole. This is the sixth, built on one rule: nothing is written on the screen, nothing depends on how long you press, nothing hooks itself, and no mistake is binary. The line is the game.*

### The line is a rope

- **The fly line is a physical thing** — a chain of points (96 by default, `/rfrod rope segments <n>`) hung off the real rod tip: gravity, drag, the water's surface, the bottom, the current. It floats, or sinks at the rate its buoyancy says; the leader is neutral; the fly sinks by its pattern.
- **It is drawn from the tip you see** — in first person the string leaves the exact tip vertex of the blank; in third person it starts on the bent 3D tip. Strand colour is the line's, darker and bluer with depth, so a sunk line looks sunk.
- **The blank bends to the rope's pull.** The load on the tip is the load on the rod.
- **Three inputs, none timed.** The mouse is your arm. **Left** pressed feeds 0.6 m; held, the hand opens and line shoots as fast as the loop pulls. **Right** clicked strips 0.6 m into the hand loop; held, the reel winds. Switching hotbar slot abandons the tackle, as on every rod.
- **What you strip hangs as a loop** between the reel and the first guide, and the shoot spends that loop first at full speed, then pulls off the reel against its click at a quarter of the rate.
- **The cast is not scripted.** Sweep back, let the line straighten behind you — the rod loads — sweep forward and open the hand as the loop passes the tip. Too early and the loop collapses; too late and the line is on the ground behind you (and can be dragged back). A fast 180° turn throws it far and slaps it down.
- **Vanilla's swing and item use are silenced while the rope is out.** A left click no longer jerks the tip; the rod GUI (Shift + use) opens only with the fly off the water.

### The take

- **A cast counts only when the fly flew** — at least four blocks out through the air and then water. A fly lowered, dropped or dragged in does not fish. Lifting the line off the water ends the drift; a fly flicked out for a moment by a strip is the same drift.
- **The landing matters.** Under 1.5 m/s the fly is a fallen insect. Slapped down at 8 it spooks the lie like a slapped lure. The splash at the fly is visible and audible.
- **Lining.** If the fly *line* — not the leader — lands within a block and a half of the fly, the line went over the fish before the fly did: the lie spooks and goes quiet, and its interest halves.
- **The lie remembers.** Water is remembered in 4 × 4 patches: every landing lowers a patch's interest a little; a fish taken or a fright halves it; left alone it rests, over about six minutes, to above normal. First drift over a rested seam is the best; the twentieth over the same metre is a long wait.
- **Presentation.** Every tied fly is fished its own way (below); a fly fished right runs the bite clock twice as fast, fished wrong the clock stalls. A dry fly dragged across the current **cuts a wake** — a V of two trails, wider and longer the faster it skates, spray when it skates hard. A dead drift draws nothing. That wake is the only thing the fish sees of a bad drift, and the only thing you see.
- **The take is the water:** the fly goes under with a bulge and a splash. Nothing is written.
- **The strike is physical:** a sharp lift — a jump of load on the blank — or a strip while the fish holds. Slack transmits nothing, so a line with a big belly has to be stripped tight first. The window is a second or two; a fish not struck spits the fly and the line goes limp.
- **The fight is the fight** every other rod has, with no drag: open the hand to give line, strip to take it, every strip a turn of the reel. The fish's runs are pinned to the rope's end.
- **Natural baits on the fly rig:** a worm, a maggot, or a live baitfish under 150 g.
- **The catch card names the fly.**

### Tackle

- **Five fly rods:** **#3** ultralight, **#5** light (the rod that was), **#7** medium, **#9** heavy, **#11** extra-heavy — the class is the rod's power in the fight and how far a swing carries the line. Every class is the hand-made blank, tinted, with its own bend buckets and 3D chain.
- **Five fly reels**, one per class; a rod seats only its own. No drag — line goes out through an open hand.
- **Nine fly lines:** three geometries × three buoyancies. **WF** weight-forward shoots well; **DT** double taper lands soft and mends best; **SH** shooting head goes furthest with no delicacy. **F** floats; **I** intermediate sinks slowly and hangs in the column; **S** sinks fast for deep holes and strong current. No weight on the line — the rod's class carries it.
- **Recipes** for all of it: the rods on the #5's bamboo pattern with the seat metal setting the class (gold nugget, iron nugget, iron, copper, gold); the reels as iron by class plus a gold nugget; the lines as a ring of string around a core that sets the buoyancy (honeycomb floats, kelp hangs, an iron nugget sinks), placed for the taper.
- **The rod GUI cannot open while the tackle is in the water.**

### Rivers run

- **River water has a current.** `Flow.at()` is the one entry point: still water and every non-river biome are zero. In a river biome each block reads its axis from the water around it, turns to agree with a per-cell world direction (the nearest ocean, else a fixed fall), fastest mid-stream and deep, slow at the banks, backwards in the eddy behind a rock, smoothed over its neighbours.
- **The world reads it too:** the water texture moves, boats, dropped items and your line drift with it; foam and bubbles ride it. A line across seams of different speed bellies and drags the fly; a small lift of the near line and laying it upstream — a mend — frees it.
- **Off switch:** `flow: false` in the config.

## Fish

### The author's table

- **267 species** (160 new), imported from the author's spreadsheet: six new families, the Afrotropical province carved out of the warm cells, a diet per species, hybrids that breed true. Every new fish has its own inventory icon.
- **A hybrid is the tank's fish** — in wild water it weighs one twenty-fifth of its profile so the sunfish crosses stop out-biting the sunfish; stocked and settled it fishes like anything else. The card names the cross by its parents.
- **Rod, rig, reel, distance and hook tolerance are retired from the catch.** The angler ladder runs 0–50 and never forbids.
- **The three river stingrays lie flat** — they were drawn standing on edge like a bream.
- **The shoal you see gates its species on the deepest water within three blocks**, not the one bank column it sampled, so the deep-water species show in the lakes and rivers that hold them.

### Cooking

- **Every fish cooks** in a furnace or a smoker and comes out the same fish — same species, weight, length, card and size on the ground. The icon switches to the species' cooked sprite (263 of them).
- **Eating feeds by weight** (2 + 6·√kg, 2 to 20). A **prime** fish gives every effect its species carries, good and bad, from `cooked_effects.tsv`; an ordinary one only feeds — and still bites back where the species does.
- **A cooked fish is dinner and nothing else:** not released, not kept in a keepnet, not put in an aquarium, not filleted, not made into live bait.

### Live bait

- **Any fish is live bait, at any weight** — the 150 g cap is gone from the crafting grid and from the hook-in-hand shortcut. The bait keeps the fish's weight.
- **A predator takes prey a tenth to a fifth of its own weight.** The bait sets a **hard floor**: minimum fish weight = **5 × bait**, no cap. A species whose biggest specimen is under five times the bait does not bite at all. A 10 kg bait is a bait for a 50 kg fish; an 11 kg fish will not touch it. (Before: 4× or 6×, capped at 60 % of the range.)
- The fly rig keeps its own limit: a baitfish under 150 g.

## Tying

*The mormyshka was the one lure in the mod that was still "a jig". Now you tie it — on the Tackle Station's **Tie** page, on a hook held in the vise, the way it is done at a real bench.*

- **The canvas is 32 × 32.** The hook and the eight templates are authored at 16 and doubled; old 16 × 16 designs scale up on read.
- **The hook is already there**, side on: eye on the left, shank across the canvas, bend on the right. You tie ON it. The station's hook picker chooses the size.
- **The palette is materials, not colours**: thread in the sixteen dye colours (string + dye), hackle (feather), dubbing (wool), an iron or gold bead (nugget), tinsel (copper), an eye (ink sac). Every pixel is a sliver of its material out of your inventory; the bench keeps a store of tying materials and shows its bill.
- **Eight patterns** — pellet, drop, devil, ant, nymph, streamer, shrimp, dry fly — ghost over the hook when picked; **Stamp** lays one down and you edit from there. Mirror and Clear beside it.
- **The drawing is the lure's icon** — in the slot, in the hand, on the ground.
- **The fish read the drawing, not the art.** Silhouette is size and weight, mean colour goes through the colour-vs-light model painted lures use, bead and tinsel are flash, hackle is action, two dark pixels are eyes. A profile can rate a fly type by name.
- **Every tied fly says how it is fished** — one gold line on the tooltip. Dry fly and ant: free drift on the surface, no tension. Nymph: free drift under the surface. Pellet, drop, devil: worked near the bottom. Streamer, shrimp: stripped in the middle layers. Fished right, the bite clock runs twice as fast; fished wrong, it stalls. Each kind sinks at its own rate.
- **The Thing.** A drawing that matches no pattern is tied with a hidden number between 0.1 and 2.0, rolled once and never shown. Fish may run from your odd creation or be unable to leave it. You find out on the water. (Renaming it was dropped — the anvil does that.)
- A tied lure is a mormyshka to every rule: the winter rod takes it, it is never consumed, it carries its own hook.

## The Pond

*A pond stops being a place you fish and becomes a place you keep — and it stops lying about what lives in it.*

- **A sign claims up to 10 000 blocks of surface** — a 100 × 100 footprint, counted as columns of water at any depth (it was 600 blocks, then 2 500, and a deep pond paid for its depth). A lake or a river still refuses.
- **Name it.** The owner right-clicks the sign with a named name tag; the tag is spent, as on a mob; nobody else can. Re-planting the sign keeps the name.
- **Read it.** Any click on the sign tells you what it stands for: the name and owner, the blocks of water, the water upgrades standing in it or on its bank, and the fish on its ledger by species — grown and fry.
- **Its own book.** A claimed pond keeps a stocking ledger of its own, keyed by its sign. Two ponds in one 128-block region used to share one: the fry stocked in one came up in the other's trap, the adults counted for both. Whatever the region's book held with a release spot inside the pond moves over on first read — a 0.9 pond's fish are not lost.
- **No checks.** In a private pond there is no habitat fit, no pair, no spawn window: the first fish or fry put in settles the species that day. Fry grow on their twelve-day clock; pairs breed by the season; the finder stops calling a hundred stocked beluga a 1 % transient. The per-minute farm tick also touches every pond signed within 160 blocks, so the pond grows while you stand on its bank.
- **Old claims load unchanged** — unnamed, the size they were flooded at, folded into columns and into their own book on first read.

### Blocks

- **The bait trap gathers live bait only** — never a real fish. It used to land a small fish of the local species a third of the time: a rod that fished by itself.
- **The five water upgrades drop themselves when broken** — they had no loot tables.
- **The aquarium's fish are visible again** — the water box is drawn after them, not before, so they are seen through it instead of thrown away by its depth.
- **The tackle station is a barrel with drawers**, facing whoever planted it.

## The Fight

- **The fish is on the line from the moment it is hooked** — the same drawing the shoal and the tank use, at true length, tail beating, the line on its mouth. A run takes it out along its course, a sounding takes it deep and dark, a breach is an arc with a splash out and back, a head-shake a shudder, a tired fish swims slower. Everyone on the bank sees it.
- **It is visible the whole fight, under water too** — it is drawn with the shoal, before the water, so the surface no longer hides everything but a breach.
- **Outclassed.** A fish whose pull is more than the line holds (margin under 0.85) is played out, not reeled: the clock stretches up to sixfold; winding into a run on a closed drag puts the line straight into overstress; the open drag during a run is how you win, almost twice as fast with the rod held across the run. The hook-up says so, once, with the ratio.
- **A played-out fish comes to the crank.** On outclassed tackle a crank gained a third; once the fish was fully tired its runs stopped and nothing gained faster than the bar bled — a beaten 238 kg beluga could not be brought the last metre. The crank's share now climbs with the fish's fatigue to a full crank.
- **The line-on-a-block snag is switched off.** It shipped in the 0.9.1 draft and was more nuisance than fight: every pier and ledge became a wall the crank could not work through.
- **Pattern runs scale with the specimen**; the fight timeout ceiling is 6000 ticks, so the giants keep every run the table gives them; a specimen's pull scales with √(weight / mean), so a 72 g barbel is not a 2 kg fight. The smallest hook on the rig names the smallest fish that can take it.
- **Two rods, two bites, ten seconds apart at least.** Several rods on one pod (or one player) no longer bite at once: a bite within 10 s of the group's last is pushed 10–100 s. Other players' rods are not touched.
- **The winter rod plays the rhythm on the jig** — hold use and the rod jigs on its own, every stop pulls the bite closer, a left click on a stop is an accent, letting go is the pause; no stroke can be jerked by clicking. The gauge replaces the jig text.
- **Fewer words:** the hook-up message, the sounding hint and the strike prompt are gone. The splash, the blank and the boss bar say it.

## Multiplayer

- **The fight's sounds are the angler's own.** The drag's ratchet and scream, the blank's creak and the crank were played into the world at one volume, so two anglers on one bank heard each other's drag exactly as loud as their own, and a neighbour's run cut the timing out of your fight. The owner now hears them at full volume at the reel; everyone else hears them at a third of the volume and a shade lower — still there, someone is into a fish next to you, never mistakable for yours.
- **Bite stagger is per pod and per player**, never across players.
- **Netting a friend's fish** (right-click on the fish at the bank) stays as it was.

## Tackle & Progression

- **The tune-up:** the long rod's bite spread is a third longer and revived lines re-clock apart; a seventh perk, Bottom Sense (fewer snags and breaks); the sounder reads 36 m and shows 16 fish; the line and card tooltips name things instead of keys; hardcore snags at 1.3.
- **Line repair:** four string around a worn line restores it.
- **The thicker-line chains are back** — mono to 0.80, braid 0.16 to 0.60, fluoro to 0.40 — after the tune-up had dropped them while the wiki went on describing them.
- **Tackle the stall sells or the bench ties has no crafting recipe** (the rods, reels and lines above excepted).
- **The quest chain asks for diets, families and weights** instead of Palearctic species; stage 7 is cold water and the fly. Twelve new achievements.

## Bugfixes

- **The winter rod casts into a drilled hole again** (since 0.9.0: the cast scan stopped on the mod's own ice hole and said *no water*).
- **A fish put back where it came out of is never "unfit for this water"** — the card remembers where it came out, and a release within reach of that spot is judged by that water.
- **The furnace no longer turns every fish into a bare perch** — it never hands a cooking recipe its input, so the result is asked of the fish in the slot.
- **The catch card names a tied fly** instead of "winter rig / mormyshka".
- **The tying canvas no longer crashes on its right edge** at 32.
- **Rope:** no kink under the tip (one owner of the tip per frame); the line no longer falls the moment it is swung (the tip is interpolated across substeps and only pulls line out when it pulls away); no NaN on a fast head turn; the sink rates of F, I and S actually differ.
- **The fly rod stops casting like a stick** (26.x: the input mixin was carried but never registered); the third-person line starts on the drawn tip on 26.2; no missing-texture fry, no missing rod blanks, no lost dye tints on 26.x; the flow crash tesselating water on 1.20.1; the cooked icons on 1.20.1 and 26.x.
- **The community hash keeps the seed's geography** when a pond takes its own book.
