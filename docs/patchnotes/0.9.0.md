# River Fishing 0.9.0

> **Draft.** New species and more features are still landing in this release; this note grows with them.

---

**Minecraft 1.20.1** (Fabric & Forge) · **1.21.1** (Fabric & NeoForge) · **26.1.2** (Fabric & NeoForge)
· **26.2** (Fabric & NeoForge).

## Bug fixes

**The beluga could not be landed.** Reported three times, and it was one line of arithmetic. A
head-shake borrows the run counters for its six ticks, and the sounding pattern's dive drain reads
exactly those counters — so from the moment a dive's cost became a share of its own length (which is
what fixed the *previous* beluga report), every head-shake was billed a whole dive as well. A beluga
shakes about every two seconds between runs: forty of them in one fight, thirteen bar-loads of drain
against a bar that holds one. Nobody was ever going to wind that back. The drain is charged for dives
only now — and there is a check that plays every species in the mod through its own fight and fails if
the bar cannot be filled, so this is the last time.

**The crash on casting a wooden rod.** Reported by three people, all on NeoForge 1.21.1: cast the stick
rod, the game dies. The line renderer kept a buffer that had already been closed underneath it and drew
the bobber into it. Only float rigs draw a bobber, and the float class is exactly the reel-less rods —
so it read as "the stick rod" when it was any float rod, every cast. Fixed at the root; the rod pod had
the same shape waiting and is fixed with it.

**A cast could land in a lake under the ground.** The scan for water walked straight down from above
your head and took the first water it met, through dirt and stone. The tackle falls, so it now stops at
the first block that would stop it falling. Ice is still fallen through, so a frozen lake still says
*drill a hole*. Known cost: water under a roof has to be cast into from under the roof.

**The rod pod hands over the right rod.** It preferred a bitten rod but could not tell a fish from a
false alarm, so an alarm that had cried wolf on rod one outranked the fish on rod three.

**Reeling no longer swings the rod like an axe.** Every crank played the vanilla arm swing and the cast
whip on top of it. A crank is a crank now; the cast still swings.

## Updates

**Journal: recipes as a bench, catches as fish.** "How to get" was a list of ingredient names; it is
the crafting grid now, slots where the recipe puts them, with tooltips, and a tag ingredient cycles
through what it accepts. A bait's catch list is the fish themselves. The six longest guide pages have
section headings — promoted from the ALL-CAPS leads that were already written, not one word changed,
the same headings in all three languages.

**The fish in the water: who is there, and how they move.** The shoal was chosen by how well the water
suits a species and never by how often it bites, so the water was full of the fish you never catch. It
is bite-weighted now; the rare ones pass through one hour in four; small fish reach the far bank as the
schools they move in instead of being cut for size. And they behave: schools keep their numbers, a fish
beats and glides, a predator that notices your bait comes to look and loses interest, carp roll and
salmon leap. The flat sprite swims with a wave down its body instead of a fold. And a fish gets up to speed
instead of changing it: the tail beat was being taken as the velocity outright, so every beat was a
step change and the shoal read as twitching rather than swimming. Speed has weight behind it now —
slow to build while browsing, immediate when something bolts. A jumper also moved twice a frame while
it was in the air, which doubled its speed for the length of the jump; it moves once. (Bodies pulled out of
the sprites exist behind `/rfrod fish3d on`, off by default — not there yet.)

## New

**Every fish carries its card.** A fish you caught is no longer three grey lines. It is a framed
card in the colour of its standing, with badges — trophy, morph, legend, prime, foul-hooked, netted,
poached — and under them: what a fisherman pays for it, its nature, where it was caught, its size
class and sex, its weight and length, what kind of fish it is and whether it belongs in that water,
who caught it, when, and on which rod. Hold Shift and the card says HOW: bait, water, time, season,
weather, the bed it lay on, the hole or drop-off it was holding on, and its four genes. The nature
— timid, wary, greedy, bold — was rolled when the fish took, and it is what you felt in the fight:
a bold fish holds the bait and thrashes, a timid one drops it.

The card is not decoration. A contract reads it to see whether the fish was caught on its terms, so
a fish can sit in a chest for a week with the rod in another one; the aquarium reads the sex and the
genes to breed; the counter reads the grade to price it. A fish netted out of the water carries a
card too, and it says so. Fish caught before this release have none, and keep the old lines.

**Contracts.** The fisherman posts three jobs a day on a board beside his counter. Click one and you
take it as a paper: *three bream from 500 g — from a river, on a float rod, at night*. The fish's own
card says whether it was caught under those terms, so the fish can sit in a chest for a week with the
rod in another one. Bring the paper back with the fish — right-click the fisherman holding it — and he
pays for the set in emeralds, XP and **reputation**. Fish in a keepnet count; the smallest that
qualify are handed over; nothing is taken unless the whole set is there. Two papers at a time, seven
days each, and every fisherman has his own board. Reputation opens a shelf at his counter that nobody
else is shown — at 5, 15 and 30.

**The fish finder is an instrument.**
- **Right-click: the screen.** The water in section along your aim — the real bed metre by metre,
  coloured by what it is made of, the bank where the water stops — with the fish standing where their
  depth is met. Click a species for what the mod knows about it here; click a blocked one for *why it
  will not bite*: wrong depth, wrong season, not this biome.
- **The bottom moves the bite.** A nudge, never a gate: cyprinids like mud and clay, predators rock and
  gravel, salmonids gravel. The species page says how the fish feels about the bed under you.
- **Hold it: the strip.** A sounder trace scrolls on the HUD while the finder is in your hand, so
  walking the bank draws the bed you walked over. Under it, a needle to the nearest hole or drop-off —
  or to the one you picked on the chart.
- **Crouch + right-click: sounding.** A marker cast five blocks wide measures the bed along your aim and
  finds holes and drop-offs. Fish hold on them; casting on one is worth a real bite bonus.
- **The chart.** Everything you have ever sounded, on one face, pan and zoom, kept on your own disk per
  world — the lake in front of you and the one you measured last week. It has holes in it until you
  cast across them.

**Seasons without Serene Seasons.** The mod keeps its own calendar when Serene Seasons is absent —
24 days a season, early, mid and late, a 96-day year — so seasonal bites, the spring restocking and
the spawning below work everywhere. With Serene Seasons installed, its calendar is read as before.

**Stocking is a job, not a dice roll.** Releasing a fish used to roll for "settled" — one chance
per fish, in the low percents, and seventeen fish to fill a pond. Now each water keeps a brood book:
release one mature ♀ and one mature ♂ of a species (the card says which), or thirty fry, and if they
live through the species' spawning window — every one of the 93 species has one now — the species
is settled here for good. The release message is a checklist: how many of each sex, how the water
suits them, how many days until they settle. Hostile water still refuses; water that only half
suits the fish takes the brood but says it will never settle. Fish the brood out before the window
closes and the book resets.

**Roe, fry, and an aquarium that is a tank.** Right-click the aquarium and it opens: six fish
slots, a food slot, a groundbait slot, a water slot, a result slot and two module slots. Put a
mature ♀ and ♂ of one species in during its spawning window, keep the food slot stocked — bait
feeds them a day, a jar of groundbait two, fish meal three and swells the clutch, fish oil hurries
the spawn — keep the water fresh (a bucket is 100%, it fouls by the day and by the fish; an aerator
module halves that), and in three days there is roe in the result slot. Take the fish out and the
roe incubates — four days, eight in a climate the species dislikes, a warm-outflow module makes it
four anyway — and you watch it happen on the tank floor: loose pale beads, then eye spots, then the
milky dead ones, then tails breaking out, then fry along the glass. How many survive is the parents'
vigour gene, plus a snag module for cover. The five water-upgrade blocks work as modules in the two
slots. Fry are an item: throw them into water to stock it, or lift them out of a stocked water with
the fry trap. Every fish's genes (size, colour, vigour, fertility) are on its card in the open, and
offspring inherit them the way Mendel said — a bloodline is a thing you can build.

**Roe and fry are goods.** Hand a fisherman a clutch of roe and he pays for it — sturgeon and
salmon roe at three times the fish's own price. He sells fry of the day's order species by the
bucket, and one post in two on the third line of his board is a fry order: twenty, thirty or forty
fry of a species, paid like a contract.

**A settled species changes the water.** Grass carp eat the weed and the tench and rudd thin out.
Silver carp clear the water and the sight hunters — pike, asp, perch, chub, trout — feed harder.
Carp muddy the bed and the salmonids leave. A settled pike keeps the small cyprinids down and the
rest grow bigger. The fish finder lists what is at work in the water in front of you.

**Water upgrades.** Five blocks for a pond of your own, counted within 24 blocks of the water:
an aerator (oxygen — the summer bite, fry survive), a snag pile (cover — predators hold, fry hide), a
gravel bed (where salmonids spawn), a warm outflow (the season comes early here) and a feeding
station (load it with groundbait, eight days at a time).

**A population has a genome.** Everything released into a water is averaged into its bloodline
and the journal shows it for the water you stand in: the size share scales the fish you catch
there, the colour share the morphs, the vigour share how fast the water recovers from fishing.
Fry released into the wild survive by the cover the water gives them.

**Nets, and what they cost.** A seine and a cast net haul fish from a water without a rod — the
seine one to three a minute, the cast net one at a time. A netted fish has no card: it was not
caught. Nets are for the pond you stocked yourself. Haul from anyone else's water, or from a wild
one, and every fisherman in the village knows: your reputation with them drops, and the water empties
twice as fast. The journal's *Nets* page says why nets are banned for anglers.

**The poaching record.** Every haul from water you did not stock is written down. Three, and no
fisherman posts a contract for you until you have put the fish back — one mature release per
offence. The journal's *Nets* page says why nets are banned for anglers, and ends on the line this
mod wants a young angler to take away: the fish you leave in the water are the fish you catch next
year.

**Fish meal and fish oil.** Three small fish — or, sadly, a bucket of fry — grind into fish meal:
bone meal for the fields and protein for the groundbait.

**Oily fish.** Nine species render down: herring, mackerel, salmon, pink salmon, sabrefish, eel,
bluefish, bluefin tuna and pollock. Smelt one or set it on a campfire and you get fish oil — the
scent in a groundbait that carries far, and now something to drink.

**Potion of fish oil.** A water bottle and fish oil in a brewing stand. It swims, because the oil
came out of sea fish: dolphin's grace and water breathing for a minute and a half. It mends:
regeneration for forty-five seconds and resistance for a minute and a half. And it clears mining
fatigue outright — the Elder Guardian's curse, lifted by a mouthful of oil. Glowstone makes the
regeneration and the resistance second level and shortens everything; redstone stretches the water
out to four minutes; gunpowder throws it, so an ally or a tamed wolf can be healed and braced from
across the room.

**The pond is a farm.** A settled species with a pair in the water grows on its own once every
spawning window, more with a fertile bloodline and good cover. A keepnet is sold to the fisherman
whole: prime fish at the market price, the rest at half, netted fish at a third. The fish finder
lists the ledger of the water in front of you — settled species, stock, brood, bloodline, days to
the next growth, upgrades in reach.

**Genetics.** Four loci on every fish — size, colour, vigour, fertility — a pair of alleles each,
the dominant one written first. Cross a mature pair in an aquarium and the fry inherit them the way
Mendel said, so a bloodline is a thing you build: an SS stock throws bigger fish, CC throws morphs,
VV survives the egg and the pressure, FF spawns larger clutches. A water keeps the average of
everything released into it, and that average is what its fish are made of — the pond you have kept
for a year is not the pond next door.

**Carp wear their genotype.** The scale cover of a carp is a real two-locus system, and it is one
here: scaled, mirror, linear, leather. Mirror breeds true. Leather cannot — the nude allele kills
the fish that inherits it twice, so a leather pair loses a quarter of its clutch and the variety can
never be fixed, which is exactly why a leather carp is worth more. Nothing you already caught
changes: mirror and leather carp keep their items, their prices and their journal pages.

**Koi are bred, not found.** The five koi become one fish with four loci — a white ground, a red
field, black markings, and the metallic lustre — and seventeen varieties fall out of the
combinations. Tancho is not a variety of its own but the cross that goes homozygous at the white and
the red at once, so every red pigment lands in a single crown spot; and yamabuki, the gold koi, is a
karasu with the lustre on — a dark ground made metallic comes up gold.

The table is read top to bottom and the first row that fits names the fish, which is why a kohaku
that happens to be pure at both loci IS a tancho. One drawing paints all seventeen: four tint layers
cut from the sprite, each handed a colour by the genotype — and, since 0.9.0, the eye is a fifth layer
that takes no colour at all, so a platinum koi no longer goes blank-eyed. The lustre only reaches your
pond through a wild kujaku or gin bekko, about one koi in thirteen; gold, platinum and tancho are bred
and nothing else. Fishermen buy koi priced by variety: a tancho is four times a plain fish, a yamabuki
three and a half.

**The pattern index.** Every carp and every koi you land carries a number, 0 to 999, and it is on the
card. Twelve families divide the thousand and each turns the fish's colour its own way; the index
inside a family shifts it further, so two fish of the same variety and the same weight are not the
same fish. Twelve exact indices are gems — sapphire, gold, emerald, jet, amethyst, pearl, ruby,
copper, jade, amber, opal, obsidian — and a gem takes the whole fish: a koi loses its patches, the
body goes one solid colour, and the counter pays six times over. One fish in eighty-three is a gem of
some kind; a named one is one in a thousand. The index belongs to the fish it paints, so it is a data
tag (`riverfishing:patterned`): a pack that wants another species collecting patterns adds it there.
It reads under Shift, beside the genes. The index is heritable and never exact: a bred fish takes the mean of its parents plus a
drift, a line breeds toward a family, and the water you farm hands its own line back to you. The
journal counts the families you have seen for each species.

**Geography, at last.** Minecraft has no geography, only weather: the same swamp, taiga and jungle
repeat to the world border, so a fish gated on biomes alone lives everywhere its climate occurs. Every
world is now cut into four faunal **provinces** — palearctic, nearctic, neotropic, indomalaya — by the
world seed, in organic regions about three thousand blocks across. A species lists the provinces it
lives in and is absent from every other one, however right the water looks: a taimen and a peacock bass
no longer share a river because the temperature matched. The sounder names the province you are
standing in. It is on the instrument bar as well, directly under the water body, so the
two halves of "why is that fish not here" sit next to each other.

**A sazan and a carp can spawn together.** The tank paired a ♀ and a ♂ of the same id, which put a wall
through the middle of one species: a carp, a mirror and a naked carp all land as `carp` and cross freely,
but the sazan keeps its own id — its own price, its own fight, it is the wild form — and so could not
spawn with the domestic carp it literally is. A profile now names the ids it will spawn with, and eight
pools are written: carp + sazan + koi; the two crucians; beluga + sterlet + sturgeon (bester is a real
fish and a fertile one); salmon + trout + char; zander + volga zander; whitefish + nelma; the four
breams; roach + rudd. The fry take the mother's species and a genome crossed from both parents — so a
sazan hen and a carp cock give sazan fry carrying the domestic scale alleles, and the other way round
gives carp with wild blood in them. Breeding a carp out of a wild sazan is now a thing you can do.

**The chart belongs to the sounder.** Every sounding used to go into one pile per world, on the
player's own disk: survey work that could not be lost, lent, inherited or sold, which on a server is
the difference between a map being work and a map being a wiki page. A fish finder now mints an id the
first time it is used to sound, carries it in its own data, and its chart is filed under it. One
sounder, one chart, and the id is on the tooltip so two of them can be told apart in a chest. Buy a
second finder and it starts blank; lose the surveyed one and the survey goes with it. The first sounder
used in an existing world inherits the pile that was already there, and takes it.

The chart itself lives in the **world save**, under that id — so a sounder that changes hands changes
hands with its bed. The first time you sound with a finder you have not used this session, the server
streams you everything it knows, in parcels, folded into whatever your client already had rather than
replacing it. Sell a surveyed sounder and you are selling the survey.

**And the chart can see them.** The sounder's bed-from-above stopped at two pixels a block — a lake
and a bit, which is no use for a question about half a continent. It pulls back to sixty-four blocks a
pixel now, twenty-seven thousand blocks across the face, and the provinces are painted underneath the
water: four grounds with a gold line where two meet, the name of the one under the middle of the chart
in the corner, and every column you have ever sounded still drawn on top. Zooming out folds the bed
down once per step rather than once per frame, so a drag at the far end is as cheap as one at the near
end. The chart is handed a scrambled seed to draw the regions with, never the world's own — a world
seed is every structure and every ore vein in it, and it does not leave the server.

The sea is not divided — one ocean, one fauna — and neither are the fish people have carried
everywhere: the common carp is on all four, and so are the grass and silver carp, the brown trout and
the largemouth bass. What that leaves is a real map: fifty-nine species in the palearctic, twenty-four
in the nearctic, eighteen in the neotropic, fifteen in indomalaya, and a reason to walk.

Two ways to fish for what is not yours. Travel to it — or bring one home: a **claimed pond is exempt**
from the gate, and a settled species already lives outside its range at a quarter of full activity. Wild
water refuses a foreign fish and says which province it is refusing on behalf of, so nobody has to
guess.

**Specialists.** A profile can now demand several biome groups at once (`biomes_require`) where the
old list meant "any one of these": a taimen wants cold AND a river, an arapaima warm AND jungle, a
snakehead warm AND swamp. Sixteen species ask for a conjunction like that, and they are the ones worth
hunting for.

**Twelve new fish.** Mullet, anglerfish, black marlin, blobfish, bluefin tuna, loach, whale shark,
nelma, ocean sunfish, pollock, red piranha, tiger shark — each with its own art, its own water and
its own way of being caught. And the ninety-three that were already here weigh half of what they
did on disk: the artwork is compressed, 7.8 MB down to 4.0.

**Four new lures.** A spinnerbait for weedy coloured water, a bladebait for cold water and vertical
work, a swimbait for the trophy end, and a wacky worm for finesse. Thirteen species answer the
spinnerbait, eleven the blade, twenty-nine the swimbait, eight the worm.

**Terralith and Oh The Biomes You'll Go.** Two hundred modded biomes now have their water named:
which of them are salt, which are fresh, which are swamps, and which are cherry groves by another
name — a sakura grove holds koi, and gets its own pond. Neither mod is required; a world without
them loads the lists as nothing.

**Stocking is easier and honest.** Release one mature pair, or thirty fry, and the checklist tells
you exactly what the water still wants. A pond you claim with a sign is yours: no wild fish move
in, the size gates go away — a carp lives in a dug pit — and the fish you put in are the fish you
get out.

**Reputation.** The fishermen keep a standing for you. It rises on contracts and falls on a net
where a net does not belong, it is a signed number on the board, and it is worked off in fish
released back into wild water.

**The cast gauge reads in metres.** The number over the bar is the distance the line lands at — the
same function the server throws with — on an oak-and-brass gauge with the unreachable band of an
under-loaded rig hatched red.
