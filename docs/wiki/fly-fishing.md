# Fly fishing

A fly weighs nothing. The line is the weight, your mouse is the arm, and nothing about it is written on the screen: the rod bends, the loop of line flies, the fly lands, the water tells you the rest. The line is a real rope — it has slack and tension, it floats or sinks, the current carries it, the ground snags it — and everything a fly angler does (the cast, the false cast, the shoot, the mend, the strip, the wake of a dragging fly) comes out of moving that rope with the rod.

> **cast → let the fly drift the way that fly is fished → the fly goes under → lift**

## What you need

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| A **fly rod** | Five classes by line weight: **#3** ultralight, **#5** light, **#7** medium, **#9** heavy, **#11** extra-heavy. The class is the rod's power in the fight and how far a swing carries the line |
| A **fly reel** of the same class | A single-action reel with no drag knob: line is given by opening the hand and taken by stripping. A #5 rod seats only the #5 reel; a spinning reel does not fit a fly rod, and a fly reel fits nothing else |
| A **fly line** | Nine: three shapes × three buoyancies (below). The rod's class carries the weight, so any fly line fits any fly rod |
| **Fly Rig** | Built into every fly rod — a tippet slot and a fly slot |
| A **fly** | Anything tied on the [Tackle Station's Tie tab](tackle-station.md) — or a **worm**, a **maggot**, or a **live baitfish under 150 g** |

The rod's own interface (Shift + right-click) opens only while the fly is off the water. With the fly in the water or a fish on, the rod is busy.

## The controls

There are three inputs and none of them is timed.

| Input | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Mouse** | The arm. Where the rod tip goes, the line follows, with lag — that lag is the cast |
| **Left button, held** | The open line hand: line runs out through the guides as fast as the loop pulls it. A press also feeds out 0.6 m by hand; held on, line keeps coming at a walk |
| **Right button, click** | One strip: 0.6 m of line pulled in and held in the hand. Held on, the reel winds in at a walk |
| **Switching hotbar slot** | Abandons the line, as on every other rod |

What you strip in hangs as a loop between the reel and the first guide. That loop is what shoots: a cast spends the hand loop first, at full speed, and only then pulls off the reel, slowly, against its click.

## The cast

Nothing is scripted. Whip the rod up and back, wait for the line to straighten behind you — you will see the rod load — then whip it forward and open the hand as the loop comes past the tip. The loop unrolls, carries, and turns over.

- Forward too early, before the back loop has straightened, and the loop collapses onto itself.
- Forward too late and the line is on the ground behind you. A line on the ground can be dragged back off it.
- The faster the tip moves, the further the line goes. A hard 180° turn throws it a long way, and slaps it down.

A cast only counts as a cast when the fly **flew**: at least four blocks out from you through the air, then water. A fly dangled, dropped or dragged into the water is not fishing. Lifting the line off the water ends the drift; a fly skipped clear for a moment by a strip is still the same drift.

**How it lands matters.** Under 1.5 m/s the fly is a fallen insect and the fish did not notice. Slapped down at 8, it puts the spot off the way a slapped lure does. You see and hear the splash at the fly, sized by its speed.

## Lines

By buoyancy:

| Line | What it does |
|---|---|
| **F** floating | Rides the surface. Dry flies, nymphs and streamers in the top of the water |
| **I** intermediate | Sinks slowly, hangs mid-water, unmoved by waves. Stripping nymphs and streamers in still or slow water |
| **S** sinking | Goes down fast, for deep pools and strong current |

By shape:

| Line | What it does |
|---|---|
| **WF** weight forward | Shoots well. The all-rounder |
| **DT** double taper | Lays down soft, mends best; shoots less |
| **SH** shooting head | The longest shoot, no finesse |

The line is drawn darker and bluer the deeper it is, so a sunk line looks sunk. A line under tension does not sink — it needs slack on the water.

## The current

Rivers move. Water in a river biome runs toward the sea, fastest in the middle and the deep, slow along the banks, backwards in the eddy behind a rock standing in the stream; foam and bubbles ride it, and so do boats, dropped items and your line. The texture of the water shows where it goes.

A line laid across lanes of different speed bellies, and the belly drags the fly. Lift the near part of the line and lay it upstream — a small flick of the rod — and the fly drifts free again. That is a mend, and it is nothing but the rope reacting to the tip.

A **dry fly dragged across the current cuts a V** on the surface. It is the one thing a fish sees from a bad drift, and the one thing you see too.

## Flies and how each one is fished

Every tied fly carries one gold line on its tooltip: how it is fished. That is the whole technique, and the bite clock knows whether you are doing it.

| Fly | Technique |
|---|---|
| **Dry fly, Ant** | Dead-drift on the surface, no tension on the line — a fallen insect |
| **Nymph** | Let it drift free under the surface |
| **Pellet, Drop, Devil** (heavy nymphs) | Work them near the bottom — euro-nymphed, or under a float |
| **Streamer, Shrimp** | Strip in jerks through mid-water |
| **The thing** | A drawing that is no pattern at all. Nobody knows how it is fished |

A fly fished the way its kind is fished runs the bite clock at double speed; fished wrong, the clock stands still. Each kind sinks at its own rate: a dry floats, a nymph goes down slowly, a heavy nymph drops, a streamer hangs in between.

**The thing** has a secret. Every fly that matches no pattern is tied with a hidden number between 0.1 and 2.0, rolled once and never shown. The fish may flee your odd creation, or they may not be able to leave it alone. You find out by fishing it.

## The take and the strike

The fly goes under with a boil and a splash. Nothing is written; that is the take.

Set the hook with a **sharp lift** of the rod — the strike is a jump in the load on the blank — or with a **strip** while the fish has it. Slack transmits nothing, so a line with a big belly needs a strip first. The window is a second or two; a fish not struck spits the fly, and the line simply goes slack.

Hooked, the fight is the [fight every rod gets](fishing-mechanics.md), with one difference: there is no drag knob. Open the hand to give line, strip to take it, and each strip is a turn of the reel.

## The lie remembers

Water is remembered in patches four blocks square.

- Every landing on a patch takes a little off its interest: they have seen it now.
- A fish taken out of it, or a fright, halves it.
- Left alone it rests back, over about six minutes, past normal to a rested lie's bonus.

The first drift over a rested seam is the best drift; the twentieth over the same yard is a long wait. Move.

**Lining.** If the fly line itself — not the leader — comes down within a block and a half of the fly, the line landed over the fish before the fly did. The lie is frightened and goes quiet. Cast so the fly lands first and the line lands behind it.

## Cooking what you catch

Any fish goes into a furnace or a smoker and comes out the same fish, cooked — same weight, same length, same card, same size on the ground. Eaten, it feeds by its weight. A **prime** fish also gives every effect its species carries, good and bad; an ordinary one only feeds, and still bites back where the species does (a barbel's roe, a koi's omen). A cooked fish is dinner and nothing else — it cannot be released, kept, put in an aquarium, filleted or made into bait.
